# 👋 Hi! My name is Tomas.

I'm a software engineer, not an ML researcher. Like most engineers, I spent the last few years watching large language models go from a research curiosity to the most important technology of my career — and found that most explanations of how they work were either too shallow ("it predicts the next word!") or assumed I'd spent a PhD reading the literature.

This is the guide I wish someone had handed me: a ~12-chapter, front-to-back readable primer on how modern LLMs actually work — from a single neuron to a frontier model serving billions of tokens a day.

A few promises about what you're holding:

- **It assumes you're a strong engineer, not an ML person.** If you know what a hash map is and remember college-level linear algebra and multivariate calculus, you have everything you need. We will use real math where it's the clearest explanation, and skip it where it isn't.
- **It only covers what's load-bearing for today's models.** There is a century of fascinating machine learning that has nothing to do with how a 2026 frontier LLM works. You will not find convolutional networks, LSTMs, or diffusion models here (beyond a sentence explaining why not). Everything in this book earns its place by explaining something about the models you use every day.
- **It links to the sources.** Each chapter ends with the handful of papers and posts that actually matter for the topic — a reading list for anyone who wants to go one level deeper.

I hope you like it!
